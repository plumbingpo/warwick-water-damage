A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Warwick Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Warwick, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

When to Call for Sewage Cleanup in Warwick

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Tubs, toilets or floor drains pushing black water back into the room
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs

Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Cleanup

A sewage backup is an extreme health hazard. Hepatitis, respiratory disease and E. coli infection are among the serious illnesses caused by parasites, bacteria and viruses found in raw sewage. Never attempt to clean up sewage without proper protective equipment. For professional cleanup, reach our team at (432) 265-3155 without delay.
Emergency sewage removal and decontamination typically takes 1 to 3 days to complete. How extensive the damage is determines the rest, and restoring everything with new materials may need 1 to 3 weeks. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Upholstery, mattresses, carpet with its pad, insulation and drywall soak up sewage and cannot be made safe, so they are thrown out. Each discarded item is documented, giving you a record to use with your insurer.
A blocked sewer line on the property is the most common starting point. Typical culprits are tree roots entering the pipe, grease hardening inside it and wipes or similar items flushed down a toilet. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement. The coverage usually runs only $50 to $100 per year and gives critical protection, so our strong recommendation is to add it. Check your policy or ask your agent.
