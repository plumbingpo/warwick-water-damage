AC Leak Water Damage in Warwick: What to Know

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. The water wicks into drywall, soaks the platform under the unit and creeps under flooring, often with mold already started by the time a stain shows. Our team measures how far it traveled, dries the building materials and fixes the damage. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ACNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Call Both TradesAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.

Coverage

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Nearby Flooring

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Ceilings Under the Unit

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Once saturated it stops insulating, and replacement is common.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.
