Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Warwick

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. We serve Warwick homeowners by tracing the full wet area and drying every bit of it, including what is out of sight.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. That is our part of the job. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. Because slow leaks give mold time to start, we also check for growth and treat affected surfaces.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the Water SupplyClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayTurn off the breaker serving the area when water is close to a light fixture, switch or outlet. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Moisture

Using a thermal camera and moisture meters, our crew checks cabinets, floors and walls and marks each wet spot, even those far from the pipe that water reached by running along the framing.

2

Make Targeted Openings

We make targeted openings to reach wet cavities, remove drywall, insulation or cabinet parts that cannot be saved, and bag any moldy material so spores are not spread through the house.

3

Dry the Structure

We keep dehumidifiers and air movers going until the subfloor and framing read normal, which usually takes 3 to 5 days. We check readings on each visit and adjust the equipment as materials dry.

4

We apply antimicrobial treatment to affected surfaces and give you moisture logs and photos. Those records show the area was dry before repairs and help with any claim.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. An adjuster may look more kindly on a leak hidden from view than on one that could be seen and was left alone. Check your policy and ask your agent. So the adjuster can work from solid facts, we provide photos, moisture readings and notes on the leak source.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • Loose or worn compression fittings and supply connections under sinks and behind toilets.
  • Pinhole corrosion in older copper lines that slowly eats through the pipe wall.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • A crimp ring or solder joint that gives out inside a ceiling or wall cavity where nobody can see it.
  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Ruined Cabinet Bases

Under a sink, a cabinet base made of particleboard puffs up and falls apart after it takes on water. They do not return to shape, so long leaks often mean replacing the base.

Hidden Mold Growth

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. During a slow leak, the back side of the drywall can be covered well before the painted face shows one spot.

Rotted Framing

Wall studs and bottom plates that stay damp for weeks begin to soften and decay. Any piece too weak to carry load gets cut out and swapped for new lumber.

Damaged Flooring

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. Laminate seams swell, hardwood boards cup, and the glue holding tile or vinyl releases.
