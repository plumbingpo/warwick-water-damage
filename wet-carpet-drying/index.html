Wet Carpet Drying: What Warwick Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. For Warwick homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch

What We Do

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Soaked Pad Underneath

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Subfloor Damage

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
