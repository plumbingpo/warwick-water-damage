Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Warwick

Think of drywall as a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Signs to Look For

  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Walls You Cannot See

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.
