Kitchen Water Damage in Warwick

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. For homeowners in Warwick, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Sink Drain Leaks

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.
