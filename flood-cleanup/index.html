Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Install a battery backup sump pump so you stay protected when storms knock out power
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers

Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Receding water that leaves muddy residue and debris behind
  • Water coming in through foundation cracks, window wells or door thresholds
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • Storm damage that lets rain in through the roof or walls

Why Flood Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

There is much more to a flood cleanup than removing the water you can see. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. This scientific approach to drying is the only reliable way to prevent secondary damage that can cost thousands more to fix.
