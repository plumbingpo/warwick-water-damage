Attic Water Damage in Warwick

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. Our team in Warwick inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Framing

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Fix the Cause

The roof or ventilation issue behind it is pointed out to you, and we are glad to pass what we found to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Under the Roof Deck

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Air Conditioner Drips

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
