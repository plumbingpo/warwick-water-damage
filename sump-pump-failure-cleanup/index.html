Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Warwick

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Stay Out of the WaterNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. Call the utility when the panel itself sits in the flooded area.
  3. Check the Pump SafelySee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Move Valuables and CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A failed check valve that lets pumped water run back into the pit.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • No backup battery to keep the pump going once storm winds knock out power.
  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Stored Belongings

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

Carpet and Flooring

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Coverage

Insurance and Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • The volume of boxes, furniture and other contents to sort, clean or dispose of.
  • How deep the water got and how much of the basement it covered.
  • If there are finished walls and floors, the tear-out of drywall, trim and carpet.
  • How long the water stood before pumping and drying could begin.

Our Process

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Make sure a functioning pump is installed again by the time drying wraps up.
