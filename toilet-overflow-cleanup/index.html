Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Warwick: What to Know

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Anything that rose out of the bowl is a different matter and must be treated as contaminated.

In Warwick, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. Clean water means extraction and drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. A stuck valve can be worked around by taking off the tank lid and lifting the float, which stops the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. If the drain line is blocked, any water you send down can come back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterWhen there is waste in the water or it came out of the bowl, keep pets and children away, and put on waterproof shoes and gloves before stepping in it.
  4. Check the Room BelowWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. Should you see drips or a sag, cut the power to the room and get belongings out from under it.
  5. Contain Clean WaterLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
Insurance

What About Insurance?

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. Months of leaking around the base of a toilet usually falls outside coverage. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Blocked traps and drain lines, which leave the bowl nowhere to empty but onto the floor.
  • Tank parts, the fill valve or the flapper, that jam and let water run nonstop.
  • Clean water flooding the bathroom from a supply line that failed or a tank that cracked.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.

What the Water Damages

Odor That Returns

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from an upstairs bathroom runs down the drain pipe and sits on the drywall ceiling underneath. Stains and sagging follow, and now and then the ceiling comes down.

Soaked Subfloor

Through the grout and around the toilet base, water works its way below tile or vinyl flooring. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

Water spreads to the vanity toe kick and the lower edge of the drywall, where it wicks upward. Several inches of wall above floor level can hold moisture with no visible sign on the paint.
